# Amazon Product Opportunity Evaluation Framework

Use this reference when the task requires scoring, filtering, or comparing Amazon product opportunities.

## Expansion Variables

| Variable | How to Expand | Typical Signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| User | New buyer group, role, age, identity, skill level | "for kids", "for teachers", "for renters" |
| Scenario | New place, event, season, workflow, gifting context | office, wedding, graduation, rental apartment |
| Demand | New job-to-be-done or anxiety | display, protect, store, install, gift, commemorate |
| Solution | New form, material, structure, kit, accessory, service | magnetic, acrylic, foldable, no-drill, bundle |

## Relationship Types

### Subdivision Track

Use when the direction deepens the current product category.

Common tags:

- Function subdivision
- Scenario subdivision
- User/object subdivision
- Form subdivision
- Material/process subdivision
- Package or kit subdivision
- Protection/storage subdivision

Example: `photo frame -> diploma frame` changes the display object and use scenario while staying inside the frame category.

### Complementary Track

Use when the direction widens the product line around the buyer's full workflow.

Common tags:

- Accessory/consumable
- Fit/connection
- Installation or process continuation
- Maintenance/replacement
- Scenario co-existence

Example: `photo frame -> picture hanging strips` solves the installation step after the frame purchase.

### Bundle/System Opportunity

Use when separate products can be combined into a more complete solution with stronger perceived value.

Example: `kids art frame + mounting strips + label cards` sells a complete child-art display system.

## Market Validation Metrics

Do not require every metric, but use available data consistently.

| Metric | What It Answers | Interpretation Caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search volume | Is there visible demand? | High volume can also mean fierce competition. |
| Purchase volume | Is demand converting? | Compare with search volume to avoid vanity demand. |
| Purchase rate | Is intent strong? | Strong rate with modest volume can be attractive. |
| Trend/seasonality | Is demand stable, rising, or seasonal? | Seasonal products need inventory timing. |
| Product count | How crowded is supply? | Some niches have many weak listings. |
| SPR or competition proxy | How hard is ranking likely to be? | Use as a directional proxy, not a final verdict. |
| Average price | Is there room for margin? | Check FBA, returns, packaging, and coupon pressure. |
| Review distribution | Can new products break in? | Low-review products with sales are useful entry signals. |
| Rating/pain gaps | What can be improved? | Translate complaints into testable product changes. |

## Scorecard

Score each dimension from 1 to 5. Use half points only when evidence is mixed.

| Dimension | Weight | What 5 Means |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Demand quality | 20% | Clear, converting, stable or growing demand. |
| Competition gap | 20% | Entry path exists through weak listings, price gaps, or low-review sales. |
| Differentiation path | 20% | Specific improvements can be expressed in product, image, copy, or bundle. |
| Profit and fulfillment | 15% | Price, size, weight, return risk, and FBA cost support margin. |
| Supply-chain fit | 15% | Existing suppliers, materials, tooling, or operations can be reused. |
| Risk control | 10% | Compliance, patent, safety, and category risks are manageable. |

Suggested decision bands:

- `4.2-5.0`: Prioritize for deep validation.
- `3.5-4.1`: Keep in shortlist; validate the weakest dimension next.
- `2.8-3.4`: Watchlist or bundle/support role.
- `<2.8`: Reject unless there is a strategic reason.

## Hard Rejection Rules

Reject or pause a candidate when:

- Compliance, safety, patent, trademark, or platform-policy risk is unresolved.
- The product cannot meet target gross margin after fulfillment and promotion.
- The direction has demand but no clear differentiation path.
- The company lacks the supply chain, MOQ tolerance, or iteration speed required.
- The candidate only changes color, size, or packaging without creating a new user, scenario, demand, or solution advantage.

## Decision Principle

The goal is not to collect 50 ideas. The goal is to use category understanding to filter 50 directions into 5 candidate opportunities, then validate 1 product worth testing.
